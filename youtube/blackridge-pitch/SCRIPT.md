# SCRIPT: Blackridge: No Way Back, the Pitch

The author's approved narration (#457-#459), read by an AI voice (Kokoro `am_onyx`); 16 beats, 748 words, 218.3 s measured.

## B00 · COLD OPEN · 15.4 s

Night. Rain on the glass. One delivery van on an empty mountain road. This is Blackridge: No Way Back, the game I am building in Godot with Claude Code. Every shot is real gameplay, played through the game's own controls by an automated player I set up to test every mission.

## B01 · BLUF · 11.3 s

Blackridge is a crime thriller set on one rainy night, lived from inside the cab of a van. When it is done, it will be a macOS build: three missions, from the title screen to the end.

## B02 · FRAMEWORK · 10.0 s

This pitch keeps two columns. Exists: what I can show you running, with the commit it ran at. Will: everything else. If you see it in this film, it exists. If I only say it, it is a will.

## B03 · WORKED EXAMPLE · 11.9 s

Act one, Night Freight. Ethan drives home through the rain, and his sister calls. Then, on the descent, a man in the headlights. He believes nobody saw. The game gives him one word: drive. That exists, at the commit on screen.

## B04 · WORKED EXAMPLE · 11.7 s

Act two, The Wrong Cargo. Watch him at bay nine: he jacks the pallet in by hand and sets it on the lift, a real job done properly. Then a container yard, a different van, and cargo he should never have carried.

## B05 · WORKED EXAMPLE · 12.2 s

Act three, Last Run. Maya hands him a rifle at the service gate. One fight at a hangar, and her daughter Lily walks out with him. It plays to the cut to black. Recording it found three bugs. They are fixed, two with a failing test first.

## B06 · DIFFERENT · 14.4 s

Why would anyone care? I have played GTA Five, where the drive is the space between missions, and Euro Truck Simulator 2, where the cab is home but nothing is at stake. Blackridge puts a thriller inside that cab. The phone on the dash is where the story calls you.

## B07 · WHO · 14.6 s

For a player, it will be one grounded night in three acts. For a hiring manager looking for a generalist game developer, which is what I want to be, the first sixty seconds will be: press start, walk to the van, drive. That is a will. My only export is from before missions two and three.

## B08 · PROVES · 15.1 s

What does it prove? The windshield and the night gauges use shaders I wrote by hand, at tag a five. The spray off the wipers and the rain in every lamp's light are particle effects, measured, at a six. Getting into the van and the ringing phone are animations with edge tests, at a seven.

## B09 · PROVES · 13.5 s

And the habits behind it: ninety-four headless tests, bugs fixed only after a failing test, frame times measured on my MacBook Air M4, and every prompt I gave Claude Code logged. Here is one of those tests, run for this film.

## B10 · FALSIFIABILITY · 14.9 s

Here is what could sink it: the frame rate. On my Air the slowest frames already take seventeen to nineteen milliseconds, over the budget for sixty a second, and no export has ever been measured. And I have not played acts two and three myself. So first I will measure the export, and play it.

## B11 · SCOPE · 15.4 s

In: one district, one van, three missions, checkpoints and one fight. Out: multiplayer, a wanted level, traffic, an open city. First: a fresh macOS export of act one, played by someone else to the first checkpoint. The save file appearing is how we will know it ran.

## B12 · WHY · 14.7 s

Why this project? Mostly learning. Godot, 3D, shaders, particles, animation, profiling, testing and directing an AI agent were all new to me this term. Six months later, in the game's own ending, the sun comes out. What exists is what I learned.

## B13 · VERDICT · 17.5 s

Let me recap. Exists: three missions you can play to the end, recorded by my automated player, and the tagged work behind them. Will: a measured macOS export, my own playtest and its fixes, a report anyone can rerun, and voices. And honestly, the game is not done yet. The story still needs some improvements.
